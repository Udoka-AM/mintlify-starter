Corridor earns where it creates value: when money settles, when it converts currencies, while it waits in transit, and on the software your treasury team runs every day. The all-in take lands at approximately **0.30% of every dollar settled** — a fraction of what the same payment costs through correspondent banks today.

## Revenue lines

| Revenue stream | Indicative pricing | Charged on |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement fee | 10–30 bps of volume (per corridor) | Every payout and collection settled through the hub |
| FX spread share | ~10 bps | Conversions priced by liquidity partners, a share passed to Corridor |
| Treasury & reconciliation SaaS | $1,500–$3,000 per month | Per customer, tiered by number of corridors and monthly volume |
| Float yield | Share of yield | Customer stablecoin balances held between payments, deployed in approved yield venues |

<Tip>
  The settlement fee varies by corridor and volume tier. Reach out to discuss indicative pricing for your specific flow — the quote engine gives you a firm rate before funds move.
</Tip>

## Unit economics: one mid-size customer

A payout company settling $5 million a month through Corridor:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|

| All-in take (settlement + FX + yield) | ~0.30% |

## Why the margin holds

The economics are durable for four structural reasons:

- **Network effects.** Each payout or ramp partner added reaches every customer and every region simultaneously. You get more corridors without more integrations, which raises the cost of switching away from Corridor.
- **Treasury lock-in.** Your ops team runs daily treasury management, pre-funding forecasts, and reconciliation on the Corridor console. That console becomes the system of record, not just a pipe.
- **Data compounds.** Pre-funding forecasts improve as Corridor accumulates volume-per-corridor history, and reconciliation logs build into audit evidence over time.
- **Cheap rails underneath.** Hub settlement costs fractions of a cent per transfer on Tempo. Cross-chain bridge hops cost fractions of a cent on Across. The 0.30% take is margin, not cost pass-through.

## Compliance posture

Corridor is infrastructure for regulated operators, not a replacement for your compliance programme.

- **You own KYC/KYB.** Your business holds the money-transmission licences and owns the KYC and KYB relationships with your end customers. Corridor does not interact with retail senders or recipients directly.
- **Corridor runs KYB on its customers.** Every operator onboarded to Corridor goes through business due-diligence checks. Counterparty wallets and sanctions lists are screened on every settlement.
- **Fiat touchpoints go through regulated ramp partners.** Every on- and off-ramp in each corridor operates through a regulated local partner. Corridor does not hold or transmit fiat directly.
- **Outbound regulated flows are reference-linked.** For flows that carry documentary requirements — such as Form M filings in Nigeria — Corridor carries compliance references in the saga data, giving your compliance team a complete audit trail tied to each settlement.

## Go-to-market sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Land">
    Start with licensed payout companies paying into Africa, beginning with the EUR → NGN corridor — the highest-volume, highest-friction flow in the diaspora remittance market.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reverse the flow">
    Activate outbound import corridors for the same customers: NGN → CNY is the first target, serving importers paying Chinese suppliers on the same integration already in place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add spokes">
    Every new ramp or payout partner onboarded becomes an additional spoke for every customer on the hub — broadening corridor coverage without requiring individual integrations.
  </Step>
</Steps>
