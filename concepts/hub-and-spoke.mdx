Corridor is a hub-and-spoke network. Every region — Africa, Asia, Europe, the Americas — connects to a single settlement centre called Tempo. A payment moving between any two regions does not need a direct link between them; it moves through the hub. That design keeps the number of integrations linear: adding a new region adds one spoke, not a new link to every existing region.

## Two primitives

Every flow on Corridor is one of exactly two things:

- **Collection:** local currency arrives through a ramp partner, is converted to USDC, bridged to Tempo, and credited to the customer's pathUSD balance on the hub.
- **Payout:** the customer's hub balance goes out to a spoke — either public (a partner pays local currency) or shielded (a Zcash Ironwood batch).

A cross-border payment is a collection followed by a payout. Because the customer's balance on the hub sits between them, the two halves can happen at different times, in different amounts, and through different partner networks.

```
          collections                         payouts
 NGN ─┐                                              ┌─▶ NGN / KES / GHS / ZAR …   (inbound remittance, payroll)
 KES ─┤                                              ├─▶ CNY / HKD / INR / AED …   (import payments to Asia)
 GHS ─┼─▶ USDC (Solana) ─▶ TEMPO HUB (pathUSD) ─▶ ─┼─▶ USD / BRL / MXN / COP …   (payments to the Americas)
 USD ─┤         customer balances · ledger · recon   ├─▶ EUR / GBP …               (payments to Europe)
 EUR ─┘                                              └─▶ Zcash Ironwood             (shielded supplier batches)
```

## The hub is enforced by the route planner

The hub constraint is a correctness property, not a convention. The route planner refuses any flow that bypasses Tempo:

```ts
// packages/routing/src/index.ts
if (purpose === "payout" && !onHub(req.source))
  throw new NoRouteError(
    "payouts are funded from the customer's balance on the Tempo hub"
  );
if (purpose === "collection" && !onHub(req.destination))
  throw new NoRouteError("collections settle into the Tempo hub");
```

This guarantee is tested in `routing.test.ts`. Because enforcement lives in the planner rather than in each leg, it holds for every existing and future lane — public, shielded, and intra-Africa alike.

## Priority flows

| Flow | Direction | Example | Payout rail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diaspora and B2B payouts into Africa | Inbound | EUR / GBP / USD → NGN, KES, GHS | Local bank or mobile money |
| Import payments to China and Hong Kong | Outbound | NGN / KES / GHS → CNY, HKD, USD | Supplier's HK or offshore USD / HKD account (onshore CNY conversion is restricted; most China trade settles in USD / HKD) |
| Import payments to India, UAE, Turkey, SE Asia | Outbound | NGN → INR, AED, TRY, IDR | Local payout partners |
| Payments to the Americas | Outbound | NGN / KES → USD, BRL, MXN | ACH / wire, Pix, SPEI |
| Intra-African trade | Both | NGN ↔ KES, GHS, XOF | Local ramps |

## Supported currencies

Corridor's currency table covers 32 fiat currencies across four regions, each assigned a stable one-byte index used in the [Corridor Reference](/concepts/corridor-reference). The table is append-only — indices are never renumbered.

| Region | Currencies |
|---|---|
| **Americas** | USD · CAD · MXN · BRL · COP · ARS · CLP · PEN |
| **Europe** | EUR · GBP · CHF |
| **Africa** | NGN · KES · GHS · ZAR · EGP · UGX · TZS · RWF · XOF · XAF · MAD |
| **Asia & Middle East** | CNY · HKD · INR · AED · SGD · JPY · TRY · IDR · VND · PHP |

Digital assets carried on-chain: `pathUSD`, `USDC.e`, `USDC`, `USDT` (all 6 decimals) and `ZEC` (8 decimals). For shielded payouts, ZEC moves through Zcash Ironwood, the shielded venue.

<Note>
The currency index is encoded directly in every Corridor Reference, so any two currencies in the table above form a valid corridor immediately — no registry update required. See [Corridor Reference](/concepts/corridor-reference) for the full byte layout.
</Note>

## Regulation of outbound flows

Outbound FX from markets such as Nigeria is regulated: import payments require a Form M and must be processed by institutions licensed for FX. Corridor remains infrastructure. The licensed PSP or IMTO you operate holds the FX permissions and the KYC. Corridor carries compliance references — invoice number, Form M number — in the saga data and produces the reconciliation trail as audit evidence.
